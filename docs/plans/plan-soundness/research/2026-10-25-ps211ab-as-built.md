# Plan-soundness: PS2.11a and PS2.11b as built

## PS2.11a · `W_WC_SIDE_CLEARANCE`

A `wc` or `toilet` whose centreline is nearer than the ruleset's `wcSideClearanceMm` to a side
obstacle. Measured square to the centreline, over the pan's depth, from the drawn footprint: the
`clear sides` strips of ADR 0029 §4, each pulled 30 mm (`WALL_COLLISION_SLACK_MM`) off the back
end so a pan drawn a little into its back wall does not read that wall as beside it.

- Obstacles: the body of every straight wall with the span of each door and cased opening it hosts
  cut out (a doorway beside the pan is a gap, measured to its jamb; a window is not cut, since the
  wall stands under its sill), and every piece at the cut plane. Not obstacles: overhead pieces
  (mirror, wall cabinet, hood), underlays (rug) and door swings (a door clears by closing;
  `W_SWING_OBSTRUCTED` judges the leaf).
- A curved wall near either side strip: the pan is not judged and the run files `curved-wc-wall`.
- Exact decision: a side is short when an obstacle is inside the strip with positive area and its
  standoff is `<` the figure less half the pan's width.
- Message: "The WC in <room label> has its centreline N mm from <the piece | a/an <kind> wall>
  beside it; a WC needs F mm to each side (S mm short)." An unlabelled room gives "A WC has…".
- Hint: the gap and the figure; then either "slide it S mm along its back wall … and its other
  side still keeps F mm" (the other side re-measured to the depth the slide needs) or "the space
  across it … is W mm, under the 2F mm a WC needs: move it to a wider run". "or move <piece>" only
  when the obstacle is a piece. No machine fix: the edit differs for `at`, `against wall … offset`,
  `in <room> anchor` and the relations, and an anchored pan has no offset to write.

Sources (`src/lint/ruleset.ts`):

|---|---|---|

| `us-residential-advisory` | 381 | IRC P2705.1, 15 in [S] |
| `uk-residential-advisory` | 400 | as the default [V] |

| `cn-residential-advisory` | 400 (the default) | GB 50096-2011 gives no figure |

## PS2.11b · `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW`

A catalogue `tall` unit (fridge, wardrobe/robe/closet, bookshelf/bookcase, locker; never a bare
`shelf`, which is often low open shelving) whose footprint overlaps, with positive area, a window's
span along its straight host, within `fixtureWallTolMm` (300) of the host's centreline on either
side. A window whose sill is at or above 2100 mm (`DOOR_HEAD`) is not judged; a window on a curved
host files `curved-window-host`. Message: "The <piece> in <room label> stands in front of N mm of
a W mm window: a tall unit against the glass blocks its light and view." Hint: the window's id
and host, the shorter slide past one of its ends, or a unit no higher than the sill. No fix.

## Shared

- Both rules are validity layer, `furniture` family, appended last to `LINT_RULES`, `invariant`
  in the equivariance oracle (no shipped example raises either code after the fixes, so the
  oracle's corpus does not exercise them: card PS2.49).
- `placementClear` (every position a lint hint quotes, and the wall-collision `offset` hint)
  refuses a WC whose side is short, a piece that would stand nearer a WC's centreline than the
  figure and no farther than what already stands there (`<=`: at a tie the new piece could become
  the obstacle the message names), and a tall unit over a window.
- `finish`'s furnish stage stands a WC it writes at a corner with its centreline 450 mm off the side
  face (the strictest of the rulesets its never-worse law holds).
- `statementExprs` (the LSP's rename and references) now lists `against <room>.<edge> offset` and
  `clear front|sides`, which the examples first used here (`test/cursor-exhaustive.test.ts`).
